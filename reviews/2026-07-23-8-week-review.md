## Three things you probably haven't noticed

1. **W28→W29 volume jumped ~28% (7.2h → 9.2h) right as fatigue was already elevated.** ATL was sitting at ~80 (well above CTL ~64) going into that week, and instead of easing off, volume increased further. That's the kind of stacking pattern — not the single week itself — that tends to precede the tendon/soft-tissue issues in the injury history (Achilles, TFL), both of which showed up during marathon-prep volume pushes.
2. **Training is almost entirely low-intensity right now.** Nearly every run sits at 118-138 bpm average (solidly Z1-Z2), well under the 173 bpm threshold. Only one session in 8 weeks (2026-07-19, 21.9 km, avgHR 150/maxHR 174) touched tempo/threshold territory. Good for aerobic base, but there's essentially no structured threshold or marathon-pace work yet — worth planning deliberately once the Amsterdam marathon "A" block starts, rather than assuming easy volume alone will get you race-ready.
3. **The detected threshold pace (~3:31/km) lines up almost exactly with your real half-marathon PB pace (3:33/km).** That's a good sign the data-derived zones below are trustworthy and not just a generic formula — you can train off them with confidence.

## Flag

Sustained ATL >> CTL for 3 consecutive weeks (W27-29), paired with a volume increase in the highest-fatigue week, is worth watching given the Achilles/TFL history. The recent cutback (W30) is the right response — just flagging it here so it's not missed if the pattern repeats during the Amsterdam marathon build.
